##### Abstract

This paper engages Langland-Hassan's argument that creativity requires imagination (CRI), arguing that the corrective half of the projective-corrective cycle belongs to aesthetic imagination. If this is right, then the capacity for aesthetic imagination requires a corrective structure that current generative AI systems lack, and this structural absence marks a philosophically significant boundary between human and machine-generated art.
