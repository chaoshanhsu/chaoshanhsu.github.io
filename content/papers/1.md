##### Abstract

Can mental imagery deliver genuine aesthetic acquaintance with a work of art? This paper develops the Corrective Feedback Criterion (CFC) and the Configuration-Response Link (CRL) to answer that question, arguing against Nanay's permissive account of aesthetic acquaintance through mental imagery. The CFC specifies a corrective structure that aesthetic mental imagery must possess to count as genuine acquaintance, while the CRL connects perceptual configurations to the aesthetic responses they support. Together, they provide a principled basis for distinguishing cases where mental imagery can and cannot substitute for direct perceptual contact with artworks.
