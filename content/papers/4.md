##### Abstract

When does a new practice become an art kind, and do the pioneering works that inaugurate the practice belong to the kind they create? This paper develops a buck-passing account of art-kind properties to argue that pioneering artworks do not belong to the kind they inaugurate at the moment of their creation. Kind-membership requires the stabilisation of a practice—a condition that pioneers, by definition, cannot yet meet. The paper draws consequences for familiar cases such as Duchamp's *Fountain* and for the general question of how art-kind boundaries form over time.
