I work along two independent lines, one empirical and art-historical, the other philosophical, both concerned with the conditions of aesthetic perception.

---

## Culture and Art Perception

Do people from different cultural backgrounds look at the same painting differently? My doctoral project investigates cultural variation in art perception using eye-tracking. I also examine the conceptual history of how claims about culture and perception have been framed across art history and psychology.

*See:* [Cultural Differences in Gaze Patterns over Paintings and Photographs](/t2/) · Oculometric Insights on Cross-Cultural Art Perception When Viewing Paintings and Photographs (in progress) · Culture and Perception: An Unexamined History in Art History and Cross-Cultural Psychology (in progress) · Dissertation (in progress)

---

## Aesthetic Imagination and Aesthetic Judgement

What must hold for someone to count as genuinely perceiving, imagining, or engaging with a work of art? I am developing frameworks for aesthetic acquaintance and aesthetic imagination that specify the structures aesthetic experience requires.

*See:* [The Corrective Feedback Criterion for Aesthetic Acquaintance](/1/) (forthcoming)

---

## Ontology of Art

How should we define art, and does the question still matter? I am interested in why the definition of art remains consequential for how we understand artistic practice and its place in society.

*See:* [When Do Pioneers Become Art?](/t1/)
